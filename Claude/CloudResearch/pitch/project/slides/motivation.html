01 / 03

After a disaster, the first satellite images are often cloudy, and clouds hide exactly the ground a flood map has to describe. In SpaceNet 8's Hurricane Ida data, 55 percent of clear roads are labelled flooded, but only 26 percent under thin cloud and 2 percent under opaque cloud. Part of that is geography, but the labelling rule matters: analysts only marked what they could see, so a flooded road under cloud reads as dry, and a model trained on those labels learns the same blind spot. In an earlier fit, our detector found 67 percent of flooded roads under thin cloud against 87 percent in clear sky. The opportunity is a flood map people can trust under cloud: see through thin cloud using a second image, and say plainly where the ground cannot be seen. Imagery: Maxar via SpaceNet 8.
