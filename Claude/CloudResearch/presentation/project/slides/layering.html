This is how the two images are layered. The haze model says a hazy pixel is the ground dimmed by t, plus the airlight A times one minus t. The dark channel, the darkest pixel in a 20 metre square, is the key: under cloud even the darkest pixel brightens. He's method, which de-hazes the after image without the before image, assumes the ground's darkest pixel is black. We have the before image, so we know how dark it really was, once the two dates' grey levels are matched with a curve on clear pixels. How much it brightened gives t. We only do this inside the cloud mask, fading out over 20 metres past it, and only for cloud blobs of at least 2,500 square metres, so a changed roof is not read as haze. Where t drops below 0.2 the ground cannot be recovered, so that part is cannot assess.
