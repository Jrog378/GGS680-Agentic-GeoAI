This is the check on the cloud masks against human cloud labels, HRC_WHU's expert masks on 120 development images. The bright rule calls a pixel cloud when it is very bright and smooth: when it fires it is usually right, precision 0.81, but it finds only about a quarter of the cloud. The dark channel looks at the darkest pixel in each 20 metre square; clear ground nearly always has one, from shadows, trees or water, but under cloud even that pixel turns bright. It finds about two thirds of the cloud, F1 0.65, but bright bare ground can fool it. Combining the two rules adds nothing, because the bright rule's cloud lies almost entirely inside the dark channel's. A trained spotter does much better: the bright rule plus texture plus DINOv2 features reaches F1 0.87 against 0.65, better on 112 of the 120 images, with texture adding the most. HRC_WHU is academic use only, so for now that spotter is research only. The synthetic-haze check shows the mask does not hide flooded roads under thin haze.
